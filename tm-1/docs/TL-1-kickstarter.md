## Start with a room

Set it on a windowsill and press record. Thirty seconds of your kitchen: the fridge, the street, a bird, the room's own hum.

Now slow it to a quarter speed. The fridge becomes a bass note. The street becomes weather. Freeze one deck and that instant becomes a chord you can hold forever — while the second deck plays the same thirty seconds backwards, drifting slowly out of phase with itself, because two tape transports never quite agree.

You have made a piece of music out of a room. You brought nothing to it.

**It's a tape recorder.** That's the whole idea.

## What it is

Two independent tape decks in one sealed steel enclosure. Each one plays at any speed from a standstill to 4× forward — or backward, continuously, through zero. Slow it down and everything slows: pitch, brightness, and wobble together, because that's what tape does.

Load audio from the SD card, from the two built-in microphones, or from the line inputs. One file or a hundred — they become a single reel up to six hours long, and you can splice a loop anywhere on it, from the whole thing down to a few milliseconds.

Then record over it. The record head has a real erase coefficient, so layers pile up and the oldest ones fade — twenty passes deep if you want. This is the Frippertronics loop with a knob on it.

There is no computer in the chain. There is no app. There is a machine with knobs, and it runs on batteries you can replace at a gas station.

## Three machines in one enclosure

**An instrument.** Freeze holds any moment as a drone you can transpose, filter and play against. Granular playheads scatter the tape into clouds. Two decks phasing against each other is the Steve Reich experiment, live, in your hands. Filter resonance goes all the way up and keeps going.

**A field recorder.** Two Primo EM272 capsules, up to 192 kHz capture, six-hour reels limited by the card and not by memory. Sealed and weather-rated — which, remarkably, no mainstream field recorder is. Record clean; mangle later.

**A room in the living room.** Leave it running and it never repeats: the two transports drift, the modulation wanders, the tape ages. It's a drone machine that makes its drones out of wherever you happen to be.

## What's actually in it

**Transport**
- Two independent decks, ±4× continuous varispeed through zero
- Real reel inertia — stopping sounds like stopping
- Splice anywhere from six hours down to milliseconds
- Loop, one-shot, spring-loaded scrub

**Tape**
- 16-tap windowed-sinc resampling, 512-phase table — measurably cleaner at extreme slowdown than anything else in this class
- Head loss that tracks transport speed, always on, because that's the difference between tape and a pitched-down sampler
- Wow, flutter, dropouts, saturation and hiss under one AGE knob — and silent at zero
- Sound-on-sound with a decay control calibrated in tape passes
- Parts-per-million drift readout between the decks, because they disagree and that's the point

**Sound**
- Freeze: hold any instant as a sustained drone, transposable through reverse
- Granular playheads from tape or from live input
- Per-deck resonant filter, master filter and output stage
- Delay and reverb, with reverb switchable between spring, plate and room
- Two LFOs plus a global modulation bus, assignable, into audio rate

**Body**
- Sealed powder-coated steel, 2.4 kg, rubber where it touches the world
- Detachable rubberised lid with the complete operating procedure printed inside it
- Full-colour screen, stereo speakers, headphone out
- Two mics, two line inputs, assignable CV and expression on 1/4" jacks
- User-replaceable batteries or USB-C
- No branding on the panel. Just the designation.

## Why we made it

Every tape instrument worth having is either a Eurorack module that needs a case and a power supply before it makes a sound, a plugin that needs a laptop, or a beautiful little thing that stays indoors.

We wanted the one you could take to a beach at 5am. Sealed, battery-powered, mic'd, with a real transport and enough tape on it to record the whole sunrise — and enough instrument in it that you could play the sunrise back as music before you got home.

Nothing like that existed. So this is that.

## Open, entirely

Hardware under CERN-OHL-S. Firmware under GPLv3. Schematics, board files, panel layouts and the complete DSP source published on day one.

Nothing inside is potted, locked or signed. If we vanish, you can still fix it, modify it, or build another one. That isn't a marketing position — it's a condition of the machine being worth owning in twenty years.

The browser version is free forever, with no account and no upsell. If you never buy the hardware, you still have the instrument.

## Risks and challenges

We'll be direct, because you're about to give money to a stranger on the internet.

**The software is real. The hardware is not yet.** Everything you can hear in the browser today is finished and tested. The circuit board that carries it does not exist yet. That is the honest state of this project, and it's why the timeline has four months in it before anyone gets anything.

**The analog front end is the hard part.** Digital processing is verifiable — we can measure it and we have. Preamps, converters and grounding are where audio hardware actually goes wrong, and they're the piece that needs prototypes and iteration rather than confidence. If something slips, it will be this.

**Component supply and small-batch manufacturing are unpredictable.** A single unavailable part can add a month. We're designing with second sources where we can, and we'd rather tell you about a delay than ship something that isn't right.

**We are small.** This is not a company with a fulfillment department. It's a person with a soldering iron and a spreadsheet, which is why the batch sizes are modest and the pledge levels are few.

**What protects you:** the design files are open from day one, so the machine is not hostage to us finishing. And the instrument itself is already free in your browser — the thing you're funding is the box, not the idea.
